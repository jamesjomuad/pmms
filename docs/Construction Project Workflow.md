# Construction Project Workflow

## Overview

A typical construction or MEP project follows a structured workflow from the time the project is awarded until the completed system is handed over to the client.

The general workflow is:

**Project Awarded → Submittals → Shop Drawings → Equipment Procurement → Mobilization → Installation → Startup → TAB → Punch List → Closeout**

Each stage has specific activities, deliverables, and approval requirements.

---

## 1. Project Awarded

The project is officially awarded to the contractor or company.

### Activities

- Contract is signed.
- Scope of work is confirmed.
- Project budget and schedule are established.
- Project manager and project team are assigned.
- Responsibilities between the client, consultant, contractor, and subcontractors are clarified.
- Project kickoff meeting is conducted.
- Initial project documentation is reviewed.

### Typical Deliverables

- Signed contract
- Scope of work
- Project schedule
- Project execution plan
- Project organization chart

### Purpose

To formally start the project and establish the responsibilities, requirements, budget, and schedule.

---

## 2. Submittals

The contractor submits technical information about the materials, equipment, and products they intend to use.

### Examples

- Product data sheets
- Manufacturer specifications
- Equipment specifications
- Material samples
- Compliance certificates
- Installation manuals
- Warranty information
- Technical documentation

### Review Process

The client or consultant reviews the submitted documents.

Possible results include:

- **Approved**
- **Approved as Noted**
- **Revise and Resubmit**
- **Rejected**

### Purpose

To ensure that the proposed materials and equipment meet the project's specifications before purchasing or installation.

### Typical Deliverable

**Approved Material/Equipment Submittal**

---

## 3. Shop Drawings

Shop drawings provide detailed information about how the approved systems and equipment will actually be installed.

They translate the design intent into installation-ready information.

### Examples

For an HVAC project:

- Equipment locations
- Duct routing
- Pipe routing
- Dimensions
- Elevations
- Equipment connections
- Supports and hangers
- Coordination with other trades
- Control connections

### Review Process

The drawings are submitted to the consultant/client for review and approval.

### Purpose

To ensure that the proposed installation is technically correct and coordinated with the building and other trades.

### Typical Deliverable

**Approved Shop Drawings**

---

## 4. Equipment Procurement

After the required equipment and materials have been approved, the contractor purchases them.

### Activities

- Request supplier quotations
- Compare supplier offers
- Select suppliers
- Issue purchase orders
- Order equipment
- Monitor manufacturing
- Track delivery
- Inspect delivered equipment
- Verify equipment against approved submittals

### Example

```text
Approved Equipment
       ↓
Supplier Quotation
       ↓
Purchase Order
       ↓
Manufacturing
       ↓
Delivery
       ↓
Site Inspection
```

### Purpose

To make sure the required equipment and materials are available when they are needed for installation.

### Typical Deliverables

- Purchase orders
- Delivery receipts
- Equipment inspection reports
- Manufacturer documentation

---

## 5. Mobilization

Mobilization is the preparation of the site, personnel, tools, equipment, and resources required to begin construction.

### Activities

- Deploy workers to the site
- Deliver tools and equipment
- Establish temporary facilities
- Complete site safety orientation
- Prepare material storage areas
- Arrange temporary power and water
- Establish site offices
- Coordinate access to work areas
- Review site safety requirements

### Purpose

To make the project site physically and operationally ready for construction.

### Typical Deliverables

- Site setup
- Mobilization plan
- Safety documentation
- Personnel assignments
- Equipment and tool inventory

---

## 6. Installation

The actual construction and installation work takes place during this stage.

### Examples

Depending on the project, installation may include:

- Equipment installation
- Pipe installation
- Duct installation
- Electrical wiring
- Controls installation
- Utility connections
- Supports and hangers
- Insulation
- Equipment connections

### Typical Process

```text
Equipment Delivered
        ↓
Equipment Positioned
        ↓
Mechanical Connections
        ↓
Electrical Connections
        ↓
Control Connections
        ↓
Inspection
```

### Purpose

To physically construct the systems according to the approved drawings, specifications, and applicable standards.

### Typical Deliverables

- Completed installation
- Installation inspection records
- Quality-control records
- Installation test results

---

## 7. Startup

Startup is the process of putting installed equipment into operation for the first time.

Startup is normally performed after installation has been completed and the necessary inspections have been passed.

### Activities

- Pre-start inspection
- Electrical checks
- Mechanical checks
- Lubrication
- Equipment configuration
- Energizing equipment
- Starting motors
- Checking alarms
- Checking temperatures and pressures
- Checking abnormal noise or vibration
- Verifying controls

### Example

```text
Equipment Installed
        ↓
Electrical Connected
        ↓
Controls Connected
        ↓
Pre-Start Inspection
        ↓
Equipment Energized
        ↓
Equipment Started
        ↓
Operating Parameters Checked
```

### Purpose

To verify that the equipment can operate safely and correctly before performance testing and balancing.

### Typical Deliverables

- Startup checklist
- Startup report
- Manufacturer startup report
- Initial equipment test results

---

## 8. TAB — Testing, Adjusting & Balancing

TAB stands for **Testing, Adjusting, and Balancing**.

It is particularly important for HVAC systems.

### Testing

Actual system performance is measured.

Examples:

- Airflow
- Water flow
- Temperature
- Pressure
- Fan performance
- Pump performance
- Equipment performance

### Adjusting

System components are adjusted to achieve the required operating conditions.

Examples:

- Adjust dampers
- Adjust valves
- Adjust fan speeds
- Adjust control settings
- Adjust airflow

### Balancing

Air or water distribution is balanced according to the design requirements.

For example:

```text
Design Airflow

Room A = 500 CFM
Room B = 700 CFM
Room C = 800 CFM
```

TAB verifies the actual airflow and adjusts the system so that the required values are achieved within the project's acceptable tolerances.

### Purpose

To verify that the installed system performs according to the design requirements.

### Typical Deliverable

**TAB Report / Testing and Balancing Report**

---

## 9. Punch List

A punch list contains items that must be corrected before the project can be considered complete.

The client, consultant, or contractor performs a final inspection and identifies deficiencies.

### Examples

- Missing insulation
- Damaged equipment labels
- Incorrect pipe identification
- Missing ceiling panels
- Paint requiring touch-up
- Loose connections
- Missing documentation
- Equipment operating incorrectly
- Minor installation defects
- Incomplete cleanup

### Typical Process

```text
Final Inspection
       ↓
Punch List Created
       ↓
Contractor Corrects Items
       ↓
Re-inspection
       ↓
Items Accepted
```

### Purpose

To identify and resolve remaining defects before final handover.

### Typical Deliverables

- Punch list
- Corrective action records
- Re-inspection records
- Punch-list completion report

---

## 10. Closeout

Closeout is the final stage of the project.

The contractor provides the required documentation and formally hands the completed project over to the client.

### Typical Closeout Documents

- As-built drawings
- O&M manuals
- Equipment manuals
- Warranty certificates
- Test reports
- TAB reports
- Commissioning reports
- Inspection records
- Training records
- Spare-parts documentation
- Final completion certificate

### Activities

- Complete remaining documentation
- Submit as-built drawings
- Submit operation and maintenance manuals
- Provide warranties
- Conduct client training
- Turn over spare parts
- Complete final inspections
- Obtain client acceptance
- Complete final project administration

### Purpose

To formally complete the project and transfer the completed system and documentation to the client.

---

# Important Note

The workflow is generally sequential, but actual construction projects often have overlapping activities.

For example:

- Submittals may continue while shop drawings are being prepared.
- Procurement may begin for long-lead items while other submittals are still being reviewed.
- Installation may begin in one area while procurement continues for another.
- Punch-list items may require additional installation or startup work.
- Closeout documentation may be prepared throughout the project rather than only at the end.

Therefore, the workflow should be viewed as a **project lifecycle**, rather than a strictly linear process.

## Simplified Concept

**Plan → Submit → Detail → Procure → Prepare → Install → Start → Verify → Correct → Handover**

This sequence provides a structured way to move from project award to a completed and accepted project.
